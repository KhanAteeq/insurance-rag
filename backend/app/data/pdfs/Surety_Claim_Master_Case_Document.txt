Key Bond Provisions (AIA A312):
  - Section 3: The surety's obligation arises after (a) the obligee declares a
    contractor default, (b) the obligee terminates the contract, and (c) the
    obligee agrees to pay the balance of the contract price to the surety or a
    completion contractor.
  - Section 4: Surety options upon default include: (4.1) arrange for the
    contractor to perform; (4.2) undertake completion itself through agents or
    independent contractors; (4.3) obtain bids for completion and arrange a
    contract; or (4.4) waive its right to complete and either pay the obligee or
    deny liability with reasons.
  - Section 5: The surety's total obligation shall not exceed the penal sum of
    $1,250,000.

A companion Payment Bond (SUR8843013, same penal sum) was also issued for this
project but is tracked under a separate sub-claim, as several subcontractors have
begun asserting payment claims. See cross-reference note in Section 14.

8. INVESTIGATION FINDINGS
================================================================================

The claims investigation, led by Sarah Whitfield with support from Meridian
Infrastructure Advisors and coverage counsel, has produced the following
preliminary findings:

Finding 8.1 — Validity of Default:
The obligee followed the contractual and A312 procedures correctly. A Notice to
Cure was issued February 10, 2026, the cure period lapsed, and a formal default
declaration and contract termination were issued March 5, 2026. The default
appears valid and the surety's completion obligation is properly triggered.

Finding 8.2 — Cause of Default:
The principal's abandonment was driven primarily by a liquidity crisis. The fully
drawn $6M line of credit, declining working capital, and $2.7M in aged
receivables left the principal unable to fund subcontractor payments and material
purchases. There is no evidence of fraud, but there is evidence of over-extension
across multiple concurrent projects.

Finding 8.3 — State of the Work:
The independent engineer confirmed the project is approximately 58% complete.
Structural steel is 75% erected but requires inspection for weld integrity after
the work stoppage exposed connections to weather. The platform canopy materials
were purchased and are stored on site (estimated value $95,000) — a potential
salvage credit.

Finding 8.4 — Subcontractor Exposure:
At least three subcontractors are owed money by the principal: Summit Steel
Erectors ($148,000), Pacific Electrical Systems ($86,400), and Riverside Concrete
& Paving ($42,000). These claims arise under the companion payment bond and are
tracked separately but affect the completion strategy, as re-engaging these
subcontractors may be more cost-effective than mobilizing new ones.

Finding 8.5 — Completion Cost Verification:
Meridian Advisors independently estimated the cost to complete at $840,000. The
surety's completion consultant is preparing competitive bids under A312 Section
4.3. Preliminary informal quotes range from $815,000 to $910,000, broadly
confirming the obligee's estimate.

Notes on Reserve Methodology:
The gross reserve of $410,000 reflects the net completion exposure plus expected
loss adjustment expenses. The undisbursed contract balance of $525,000 is a
critical offset: under the A312 bond, the surety is entitled to the remaining
contract funds when it completes the work, so only the shortfall above that
balance represents true completion loss.

The anticipated recovery of $300,000 is based on the General Indemnity Agreement
(see Section 13) signed by the principal and two personal indemnitors, plus an
estimated $95,000 salvage value in stored canopy materials. The net reserve
position of $110,000 assumes moderate recovery success; a conservative scenario
with no recovery would raise the net reserve to $410,000.

Reserve Sensitivity Analysis:
  - Best case (full recovery, low completion cost):   Net loss ~$45,000
  - Expected case (moderate recovery):                Net loss ~$110,000

16. ADJUSTER NOTES & STRATEGY
================================================================================

Adjuster: Sarah Whitfield
Note Date: March 30, 2026

This is a textbook A312 performance-bond default with a valid termination and a
cooperative obligee that prefers completion over tender. My recommended strategy
is to elect completion under Section 4.3 (obtain bids and arrange a completion
contract) rather than tender the penal sum, for three reasons:

  1. The obligee strongly prefers completion due to the FTA grant deadline, and
     good-faith completion mitigates bad-faith exposure.
  2. Completion allows the surety to control costs and apply the $525,000 contract
     balance directly, whereas a straight tender of the $1,250,000 penal sum would
     be far more expensive.
  3. Re-engaging the existing subcontractors (Summit Steel in particular) may be
     cheaper and faster than full re-mobilization, and helps resolve the parallel
     payment-bond claims.

The key risks are: (a) the 30-day statutory deadline (~April 9) — we must send a
written election letter before then; (b) completion cost overruns above the
$840,000 estimate; and (c) the principal's liquidity crisis limiting near-term
indemnity recovery. I have drafted the collateral demand and am awaiting manager
approval.

Overall, I project a net loss in the range of $110,000 (expected) with strong
mitigation from the contract balance, salvage, and GIA. I have rated this claim
HIGH severity due to the penal sum, the statutory deadline, and the bad-faith and
litigation exposure, warranting executive notification.
